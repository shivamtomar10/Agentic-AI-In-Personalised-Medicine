How a check-in works

  1. You share new results

    Labs, medicines, allergies and a few words on how you feel. Words like "chest pain" trigger an immediate prompt to seek urgent care.

  2. The system compares with your history

    Rules decide whether anything moved by more than normal measurement variation. If not, earlier reasoning is reused. If so, the affected checks run again.

  3. A clinician decides

    Suggestions wait in a review queue. Each one links to its evidence. Only a clinician's sign-off turns a suggestion into a decision.

What it will not do

  • Act without a clinicianEvery output carries a "requires clinician review" state until a named clinician approves, modifies or rejects it.
  • State a claim without a sourceA claim with no retrievable evidence is withheld from the approved output and shown to the clinician as withheld.
  • Pretend to be certainWhen confidence is low, the system defers instead of recommending, and says why.
  • Hide what happenedReviews and system events go into a hash-chained audit log, so tampering is detectable.
